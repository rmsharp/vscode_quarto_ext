# Which open backlog items touch a document a human wrote

**Audit, Session 251 (2026-08-25).** Workstream:
`docs/methodology/workstreams/AUDIT_WORKSTREAM.md`.

Operator's question, verbatim: *"Sort the 192 BL items by whether they affect real documents
versus enumeration-only, so I can see how many actually matter."*

---

## Audit summary

| | |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | All **192 open items** in `BACKLOG.md` (156 "Up Next" + 36 "Polish / deferred"). |
| **Criteria** | For each item: does the construct it describes occur in a document a **human authored**, and where it does, does the shipped `findHeadings` actually disagree with what quarto renders? |
| **Instrument** | A new **3,383-document corpus of authored `.qmd`/`.Rmd`** (median 112 lines), built this session — plus a full `quarto render` of every one of them. |
| **Coverage** | **192 of 192 items classified, 0 unassigned.** 147 items carry a calibrated detector; every detector was re-run by this session rather than taken on report. |
| **Finding count** | **34** items reach real documents · **90** are enumeration-only · **68** are not document items at all. Plus **1 new unfiled defect**, **1 unsound instrument**, and **1 corpus-provenance finding** that reframes the other 191. |

### The three headline corrections to the premise

1. **The "corpus" this project has swept for ~70 sessions contains no real documents.** All
   60,998 paths in `scratchpad/s249/corpusfiles.txt` live under `scratchpad/` and are prior
   sessions' own generated probe output. Median document: **51 bytes, 5 lines.** So the nine
   items that record *"corpus weight ZERO"* were measured against generated probes, and that
   phrase has never meant "does not affect real documents."

2. **Under measurement the ranked list inverts.** Four of the five candidates Session 250
   handed forward — including its **#1 recommendation** — occur in **0 of 3,383** authored
   documents. The item that reaches the most real documents was not on the list at all.

3. **The model is right on 94.91% of authored documents, and where it is wrong the cause is
   overwhelmingly ONE family the backlog under-weights** — Quarto's own `:::` divs. Callouts
   and tabsets account for the bulk of the 124 divergent documents; the setext/list-marker
   grammar that consumed most of the last 15 sessions accounts for almost none of them.

## The instrument, and why a new one was needed

### What the inherited corpus is

Verified firsthand by reading it, not by trusting its name:

| | inherited "corpus" | this session's real corpus |
|---|---|---|
| documents | 60,998 | 3,383 |
| every path under `scratchpad/` | **yes — all of them** | no |
| median size | **51 bytes / 5 lines** | **112 lines** |
| p90 lines | 13 | 464 |
| provenance | generated by sessions s181–s246 | authored by humans |

The project does have a **real-document control** — `scratchpad/s249/repofiles.txt`, 114 files
— but it is 69 of the project's *own* methodology `.md` files plus 45 of its *own* `.qmd` test
fixtures, and it is used as a **regression** control ("did my change move it?"), not as a
**prevalence** measure ("does this defect occur in the wild?"). Those are different questions
and only the second one is the operator's.

### How the real corpus was built

`find` over the user's machine for `.qmd`/`.Rmd` outside this repo's scratchpad, fixtures and
`.vscode-test`: **4,910 files** → **3,383 kept**. Dropped: 1,467 byte-identical duplicates
(backup checkouts), 53 cloud-storage stubs that block forever on read, 3 unreadable, 4 empty.

Composition: 1,332 `.qmd` + 2,051 `.Rmd`; 2,283 from the user's own projects, **602 from
quarto-web** (Quarto's own documentation site — the best available sample of documents that
exercise Quarto's full feature set), 456 R-package vignettes.

Build script: `scratchpad/s251/build_corpus.py`. Reads run in a **subprocess under `timeout`**,
not in-process: a cloud-storage placeholder answers `getsize()` instantly from metadata but
blocks forever on read, and an in-process read cannot be interrupted — a thread pool starves
after its worker count is consumed and every later file then "times out" spuriously. The first
attempt hung on exactly this.

### Limits of the instrument — stated up front

- **Effective independence is lower than 3,383.** Only **2,026 distinct basenames**: quarto-web
  is checked out twice, `index.Rmd` appears 200 times. Deduplication was by content hash, so
  these are near-duplicates, not copies — but they are not 3,383 independent authors.
- **It is one machine's corpus**, weighted toward R-centric scientific writing and one
  quarto-web checkout. It is not a random sample of world Quarto usage.
- **942 of 3,383 produce no quarto ground truth** (see finding 7).

## Findings

### F1 — Nine items' "corpus weight ZERO" measures probes against probes

All nine cite their corpus size in the item text, and the sizes are exactly the probe-corpus
sizes: *"60,998 documents"*, *"51,382 documents"*, *"49,193 documents"*. None of the nine was
ever checked against a document a human wrote.

**This is not a criticism of those measurements** — they are real and they were correctly
labelled as sweeps. It is a criticism of how the phrase reads at triage time. "Corpus weight
zero" is routinely the deciding sentence in a ranking, and it does not carry the information a
reader takes from it.

**Recommendation:** rename the field. `probe-corpus weight` costs one word and removes the
ambiguity permanently.

### F2 — The ranked list inverts under measurement

Session 250's handoff ranked five candidates. Measured against 3,383 authored documents
(detectors in `scratchpad/s251/det/`, each calibrated against a known-positive control before
its zero was believed):

| Session 250's rank | item | real documents |
|---|---|---:|
| **#1 (recommended)** | L52 — empty-item stack then `---` is a thematic break | **0** |
| #2 | L54 — setext title still pushes its list content column | **0** |
| #3 | L58 — the declined container-pop half | **0** |
| #4 | L60 — prose token + block quote invents an ATX heading | **0** |
| #5 | L62 — `LIST_MARKER_PREFIX` misses `a.`/`iv.`/`(1)` div fences | **0** |
| *(not ranked)* | **L56 — letter/roman markers under nine readers** | **82** |

L52's own record already said *"corpus weight ZERO"*; this audit adds that it is zero on real
documents too, by an independent instrument. The adversarial pass re-attacked all five with its
own scans — never reusing the first agent's detectors — and upheld every zero.

⚠ **L56's 82 is construct presence, not confirmed breakage.** Its letter/roman lists are
genuinely there — verified by eye in `nprcgenekeepr/vignettes/manual_components/_breeding_group_algorithm.Rmd`,
a real nested `a./b./c.` + `i./ii./iii.` outline — but the *divergence* it describes additionally
requires a non-default reader, and only **4 of 3,383** documents set a `from:` key at all
(two documents, each checked out twice: `markdown+emoji` and `markdown-grid_tables`).
Construct presence is an upper bound on impact throughout this report.

### F3 — On authored documents the shipped model agrees with quarto 94.91% of the time

Every one of the 3,383 documents was rendered through the real `quarto` CLI (`--to html
--no-execute`, isolated temp directory, nothing written into the user's own folders) and the
rendered heading list compared against `findHeadings` — which is literally
`scanRegions(text).headings.slice()`, the exact array the Outline provider consumes
(`src/providers/outline.ts:103` → `buildOutline` → `scanRegions`), so this measures the surface
the user actually sees.

| | |
|---|---:|
| rendered | 3,383 |
| quarto exit 0 | 2,441 |
| excluded — `{{< include >}}` (quarto expands the child, the model reads one file) | 3 |
| **scored** | **2,438** |
| structural agreement | **2,314 (94.91%)** |
| **structurally divergent** | **124** |

Of the 124: **81 phantom** (the Outline shows entries quarto does not render — 217 extra
entries), **34 lost** (34 documents missing 157 entries a reader would expect), **9** same count
at wrong levels.

⚠ **STRUCTURE is scored, not text.** The model reports a heading's *source* text and quarto its
*rendered* text — an already-filed family (L204/F02). 242 further documents differ in text
only, almost all of it smart quotes (`'` → `’`) and stripped emphasis. Those are not counted as
structural divergences.

### F4 — The dominant real-document cause is Quarto's own `:::` divs, not the setext grammar

Joining the 147 calibrated detectors against the 124 divergent documents
(`scratchpad/s251/attribute.py` — presence, which narrows the candidates; it does not
adjudicate between them):

| item | divergent documents whose text contains its construct | direction |
|---|---:|---|
| **L268** — a fenced div / callout is a container `contentColumns` never pushes | **52** | all phantom |
| **L180** — a heading inside a CALLOUT is the callout's *title*, not a heading | **50** | all phantom |
| L447 — indented 4-space code blocks as a skip region | 22 | mixed |
| L204 — multi-line heading, source-vs-rendered text | 4 | mixed |
| L256 · L56 · L70 · L276 · others | ≤3 each | mixed |

Confirmed by reading the document: `wsfct/web/docs/articles/intro-for-leaders.qmd` contains four
`::: {.callout-*}` blocks, each with a `##` heading inside — `## Zero Trust Architecture`,
`## Data Flow`, `## No Staging Environment`, `## See Also`. Quarto consumes each as the
callout's title; the Outline shows all four as phantom entries.

**These two items are filed and neither was ranked.** L180 and L268 sit unranked in "Up Next"
while the top of the list is a family with zero real-document reach.

### F5 — NEW, UNFILED: headings inside a `.panel-tabset` are tab labels, and the model reports them as headings

**16 divergent documents were explained by no filed item. 11 of the 16 contain a
`::: {.panel-tabset}`.**

Minimal pair, both halves measured firsthand:

```markdown
## Real Heading

::: {.panel-tabset}

## Tab A

content a

## Tab B

content b

:::

## After
```

| | |
|---|---|
| **quarto renders** (real CLI) | `h2:Real Heading` · `h2:After` |
| **this model reports** | `h2:Real Heading` · `h2:Tab A` · `h2:Tab B` · `h2:After` |

Two phantom Outline entries per tabset. `grep -i tabset BACKLOG.md` returns **nothing**;
`grep -ri panel-tabset src/` returns **nothing** — the model has no concept of the construct.

It is the same *family* as L180/L268 — a Quarto div that consumes headings, keyed on the
div's CLASS rather than its syntax — but it is not the same rule, and neither filed item
covers it:

| | L180 / L268 (filed) | this finding (new) |
|---|---|---|
| class | `.callout-*` (and raw `<div class="callout">`) | `.panel-tabset` |
| which headings are consumed | the **first** one, lifted into the title bar | **every** one, each becoming a tab label |
| a 2-heading block | one consumed, the rest render | **all** consumed |

So a fix derived from L180's "first heading → title" rule would still leave every tab after the
first as a phantom. It is invisible to enumeration because a 5-line probe never builds a tabset.
Filed by this session as a new backlog item.

### F6 — The inherited HTML extractor is unsound on authored documents (fifth in a documented family of four)

`render250.sh`'s scoring column `H_DIRECT` matches `<hN ...>text</hN>` where *text contains no
`<` at all*. That is sound for probe corpora, whose heading text is always a bare literal. On
authored documents it fails in **both** directions, proven on one file
(`comparisons/hermes-methodology/methodology-vs-hermes-agent.qmd`):

- **drops** `## A concrete \`SKILL.md\`` — the `<code>` span contains `<`, so a heading that
  renders is scored as absent (the heading-losing direction, exactly as the four defects already
  documented in that script's own header);
- **invents** `Contents` and `Other Formats` — quarto's TOC and its margin-sidebar
  alternate-formats block are real `<h2>`s belonging to no source line.

This session's replacement (`scratchpad/s251/extract_headings.py`) uses a real HTML parser and
skips page chrome by container class/id. **The correction matters to the headline number:**
divergences went **272 → 151 → 136 → 124** as three artifacts of my own instrument were found
and removed —

1. quarto's page chrome (`Other Formats`, TOC) counted as headings the model "lost" — **121 documents**;
2. blind tail-stripping of `References`/`Footnotes`, which deleted headings authors really wrote — **15 documents**;
3. skipping `id="footnotes"`, which is *also* what quarto names an author's own `## Footnotes` section — **12 rows**.

Every one pointed the same way: making the model look worse. The 124 is what survived.

### F7 — 41% of the real `.Rmd` corpus has no quarto ground truth at all

942 of 3,383 documents fail `quarto render`. The dominant cause is not a defect in this
extension: **1,395 documents (41.2%)** carry an rmarkdown-style `output:` key that quarto's
front-matter validation rejects, and 168 are `_`-prefixed include fragments that are not
standalone documents.

That is worth stating plainly because the extension **does** provide an Outline for those files
(the `.Rmd` scope shipped in Session 170). For 41% of real R Markdown documents there is no
oracle — the Outline is the only structure the user ever sees, and no render can check it.

### F8 — A real document makes `quarto render` crash with SIGSEGV

`.../library/broom/doc/bootstrapping.Rmd` returns **exit 139**. Backlog item L377 records this
crash class from a generated container document; this is a real-world instance of it, in a
published CRAN package's vignette.

## Method, and what each step is worth

1. **Built the real corpus and validated it firsthand** — read sample documents from both
   corpora rather than trusting path names.
2. **192 items classified by a 12-batch fan-out**, each batch writing a calibrated detector per
   parser item. `scan.py` **aborts** rather than print a zero when a detector does not fire on
   its own control, because a broken detector's zero reads exactly like "never touches a real
   document" — the dangerous direction of this whole audit.
3. **Every `PROBE_ONLY` verdict was adversarially attacked** by a second agent instructed to
   refute it, forbidden from reusing the first agent's detector. Result: **6 overturns**, each
   with a named document.
4. **Re-ran all 147 detectors myself.** 7 counts disagreed with what the agents reported; my
   re-run governs, which moved **5 verdicts** (3 down from `REACHES_REAL`, 2 up). The
   disagreement is recorded in `final.json` rather than smoothed away.
5. **Rendered all 3,383 documents through the real quarto CLI** and scored structure, after
   replacing an extractor that was unsound on authored text (F6).
6. **Joined detectors against divergent documents** to attribute causes, then **read the
   documents** behind every attribution that appears as a claim here.

### Two of this audit's own hypotheses were wrong and were caught by checking

- **196 documents where the model reports zero headings despite `#`-shaped lines** looked like
  mass heading loss. It is correct behaviour: they are `.Rmd` files that are one big R chunk, and
  every `#` is an R comment. Checked before reporting.
- **A `\x01` in the model's heading text** looked like a control character leaking into the
  Outline. It was my own census writer's separator. A probe against `findHeadings` itself
  (`scratchpad/s251/soh.test.ts`) showed 0 of 22 headings carry it.

## Recommendations

1. **Rank by real-document reach, not by enumerated document count.** The two orderings are
   close to uncorrelated: the largest enumerated family (L52, 8,323 enumerated documents) is 0
   real documents; the largest real family (callouts, 50+) has no enumerated headline at all.

2. **Take the `:::` div family next — L180 and L268 together.** They are the dominant real cause,
   both filed, both unranked, and they share a mechanism (a Quarto div that consumes or contains
   headings). The new panel-tabset item belongs in the same slice's design even if not its code.

3. **Rename `corpus weight` to `probe-corpus weight`** in `BACKLOG.md`'s conventions, and add
   real-document reach as a separate field for new filings.

4. **Adopt the real corpus as a standing gate.** `scratchpad/s251/realfiles.txt` plus the cached
   renders (`scratchpad/s251/htmlcache/`, 28 MB gzipped, 2,441 documents) make the full
   comparison a sub-minute re-run — no quarto invocation needed unless the corpus changes. A
   session that ships a model change can now answer "did this move a real document?" cheaply.
   ⚠ The corpus lives outside the repo and is machine-specific; the list, not the documents, is
   what a future session inherits.

5. **Retire or downgrade the 90 `PROBE_ONLY` items deliberately, not silently.** They are not
   worthless — they are correctness work on constructs pandoc genuinely supports. But they should
   compete for sessions against work that changes what a user sees, and right now they are
   winning that competition by default.
